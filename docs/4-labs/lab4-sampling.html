0.1 Big Picture

In this lab you will estimate a population parameter about some population of islanders using a stratified random sample design.

1 Determine Research Question and Population (Checkpoint 1 – due Wed 11/4)

Create a research question where the parameter of interest is a population proportion regarding a population of islanders.

The requirements of your question are:

  • it must be about a population proportion
  • islanders are the observational units AND sampling units
  • be specific about your population
    • coverage error should not be concern in your study, so choose your population wisely
    • it is going to be DIFFICULT to have your population be all islanders
    • your population should have at least 300 islanders in it (\(N \geq 300\))
  • it should not be about age, income, or which city the islander lives in since you will be collecting those variables anyway
  • you may not look at weight or classify food as “junk food” – I don’t want to read that
WarningConsider your population carefully!

Note that you need to be able to have a FULL SAMPLING FRAME of islanders for whatever population you choose. So make sure you choose a population that you can reasonably do this for, given the setup of the Islands Simulation!!

You might want to check out these buildings in each city to help with making a sampling frame: Hall, Bureau, Clinic, School, Hospital, University…

You should think about this BEFORE determining your strata and maybe even your question!

TipHave fun!

This will be more fun and interesting for you if you are creative!

I recommend you look at the “Survey” option in Islands – there are a lot of interesting questions you can ask your islanders in addition to the documentation that is already there about them.

For Checkpoint 1 you will write a short proposal for your study specifying:

  1. A specific research question referring to the parameter of interest and the population you are interested in.
  2. How you will measure the outcome you identify (do you need to give a survey or is the information already in the islander information?)
  3. How you plan to construct a sampling frame (does not need to be highly detailed, but give me an idea).
  4. What strata you plan to use noting what each stratum is and how many there are.

2 Construct your Sampling Frame

The first step is to construct your sampling frame

  • Your sampling units should be one islander
  • You should not have any coverage error in your study
  • Make sure you note the strata in your frame!

I would recommend doing this in a shared Excel or Google Sheet

3 Determine the Sample Size

  • Estimate the maximum overall sample size \(n\) given some bound of error on your estimate
  • You can choose your bound of error, but it should be smaller that 0.06
  • After determining the overall sample size, use one of the allocations we learned (Proportional, Neyman, or Optimal) to find the sample size for each stratum (\(n_1\), \(n_2\), …)

5 Data Collection

WarningGet on the same page before jumping in

As with all of the Labs, collecting data is the most time consuming and tedious part! You don’t want to have to redo this!

Make sure everyone in your group has read the instructions here FULLY before starting. Make sure that you are all on the same page with a plan about how you are going to complete the data collection and divide up the work.

Note: You definitely should divide up this work but everyone in your group is ultimately responsible for all of the data quality!

  • Put your the sample data you created in Step 4 in a sharable excel sheet or google sheets to record your data
  • remember that each line in your dataset should represent one islander
  • columns in your file should be named appropriately
    • think about what easy but clear names will be for analyzing this data IN R
  • collect information about your sample of islanders from The Islands including:
    • your variable(s) of interest being measured to answer the research question
    • island the islander lives in (Ironbard, Providence, or Bonne Sante)
    • city/village the islander lives in
    • household number
    • age
    • monthly income
    • whether the islander owns a pet
    • whether the islander gave consent or not
      • if they did not give consent to be in your study, you should still be able to measure everything but your variable of interest!
      • you will use this information for a nonresponse analysis

As you collect data, make sure you are recording information about:

  • how each response was coded in the data sheet
  • how will missing data is handled
  • people who refused to respond and people who could not be reached
  • any codes / acronyms used in the recording of the data
  • anything else that did not go to plan, like participants dying etc.
