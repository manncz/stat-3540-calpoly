2 Power Analysis

Conduct a power analysis to determine the required sample size for your experiment. To do this:

  1. Collect a small scale pilot study (you can just pick some “pilot Islanders”). Think \(r = 2\) or \(r = 3\).
    • If using the example RQ, you can use Dr. Robinson’s pilot data here.
  2. Determine the practically significant difference (\(\delta\)) that is relevant to your research question.
    • Past literature? What you saw in your pilot study? etc.
  3. Estimate experimental variance using your pilot data set. (\(\sigma^2\))
  4. Calculate the sample size (i.e., \(r\)) necessary to reach 80% power.
  5. Document your calculations and reasoning in your report.

3 Random Sampling and Treatment Assignment

Using your list of 60 Islanders:

  • Randomly sample the required number of participants based on your power analysis. If your power analysis suggests a sample size larger than 60, use all 60 participants and make a note about it (e.g., although our power analysis indicated xx, we did not have enough funding to x).
  • Randomly assign treatments to participants in a CRD using the tools in R learned in class.
    • See CRD Randomization slides.
    • You may either use edible or do it “by hand” but either way you will turn your code in.
    • Add a column to your dataset that specifies each participant’s treatment group and a blank column for adding the response data.
    • Use context as your column headers (e.g., chocolate_percent instead of treatment).

4 Data Collection

Big picture: utilizing the “Task” button on the Islands:

  1. apply your treatment appropriately
  2. record the response variable
  3. fill out the response column in your data set.

Step 1: Determine your data collection procedure

  • Think about direct controls!
  • Talk about and solidify the data collection procedure as a group BEFORE COLLECTING ANY DATA to ensure you are following the same processes (e.g., time between eating chocolate and measuring Blood Glucose).
  • Write down your procedure before anything else
  • Be careful about timing!!
    • Should you measure your response immediately after applying the treatment or allow for some time to pass?? Be careful about real versus island time
    • Remember your Islanders will sleep and this will take time.
  • Be thoughtful and careful and work together! Communication is seriously key here.

Step 2: Implement treatment and collect response

  • Once you implement the “Task” you can find it in your “Recent Tasks” section.
  • Follow the procedure you determined as carefully as you can.
  • If you accidentally mess up data collection (e.g., have them eat 2 chocolate bars), this will mess up your data and you should make a note/possibly remove this observation.
  • Carefully record your data collection as it actually happened and note any deviations from your original planned procedure as you implement your design and treatment.

5 Data Analysis

Your analysis of the data should include the following.

Where you will be using R to analyze the data is noted with the R logo.

5.1 Statistical Effects Model

  • Provide the equation for the effects model.
  • Describe the parameters in the model.

5.2 EDA (Exploratory Data Analysis)

  • Create side-by-side boxplots of the response by treatment group (with individual data points overlayed)
  • What are your initial impressions? Do there appear to be differences between groups? How does the within-group variability seem to compare to the between-group variability?

5.3 ANOVA Table

  • Conduct a full ANOVA in R
  • Report the table and draw conclusions from the F-test.
  • Who can you generalize your results to?

5.4 Estimated Parameters

  • Estimate model parameters in R (sum to zero constraint)
  • Present nicely in a table
  • Discuss their interpretation in paragraph form.

5.5 Least Squares Means

  • Estimate and create a graph of least squares means (and SEs) in R.
  • Create a nicely formatted table of least squares means in report.
  • Interpret results: Identify the highest and lowest treatments and discuss practical implications.

5.6 Contrasts / Pairwise Comparisons

  • Conduct at least one meaningful contrast OR pairwise comparisons with a multiplicity adjustment.
  • Clearly define the contrast if going that route.
  • Interpret the findings in context of your research question.

5.7 Residual Analysis

  • Assess model fit and model assumptions with residual analysis plots.
  • Is the inference valid? (i.e. do you trust the SE’s and p-values)
