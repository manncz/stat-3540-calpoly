Scenario

To investigate the impact of a new algebra curriculum and different instructional methods on math skills on students in Texas, researchers randomly selected 20 high schools in Texas (all from different districts and that had at least 4 algebra classes at the school in a semester).

Within each school, four algebra classes were chosen, with each assigned to a unique combination of algebra curriculum and instructional method. The algebra curriculum included (1) a new online tutor-assisted curriculum (novel tutor) and (2) a standard curriculum for Texas high schools (standard). The instructional methods (1) teacher-led non-interactive lectures (non-interactive), and (2) teacher-led interactive lectures with group activities (interactive).

This resulted in a total of 80 classrooms, each with approximately 30 students, yielding a total of 1,800 students in the study. At the end of the school year, all students took standardized math test scored on a 0 to 100 scale. We also have their score on the test from the year before they took algebra.
